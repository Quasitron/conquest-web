# Playtest notes — 2026-10-15 (Milestone 2a build)

The user's notes from the first sessions on https://theaterofconquest.com, grouped by where they will likely land.

## Balance (Milestone 2b, first item; decided)
- **Neutral cities are too hard to take.** The current odds are 50% per army: city defense 2 against army attack 2. The user lost 3 armies on their first city.
- **Decision:** add this as a **house rule game option**, "Easier neutral cities". When it's on, neutral cities defend at half strength (defense 1), about a 67% capture chance per attack. When it's off (the default), all cities defend at 2 (50%), as in the original. Enemy-owned cities are always 50%.
- **Implementation:**
  - Add a `rules` object to `GameState`, set at `createGame` and shown on the New Game screen under "House rules".
  - In `attackCity`, use defense 1 when the option is on and `city.owner === null`.
  - Test both settings.

## Principle: original rules by default; changes are house-rule options (decided 2026-10-15)
- Game rules follow the original Empire Deluxe manual by default.
- Any rule that differs from the original becomes an optional **house rule** that players turn on at game setup. Defaults stay classic.
- **2b design task:** review the existing differences below and decide for each whether it becomes an option, is restored to the original, or stays as it is because it's purely technical.
  1. **Turn-1 production:** no city makes production progress on turn 1, to keep the first turn fair.
  2. **Elimination timing:** eliminations are checked for every player at the end of every turn. The original checks only at the end of the eliminated player's own turn.
  3. **Aircraft end-of-turn:** an aircraft can't end its turn on an occupied square. The engine enforces this by refusing End Turn.
  4. **Multiple turns per seating:** the original's "several turns per seating while far apart" rule was dropped.
  5. **No survivors:** a game where everyone is eliminated at once ends with no winner. The original has no equivalent.
  6. **Unit stats:** they match the manual's Standard-rules values, so no change is needed. Re-check them when the Advanced rules arrive.

## Turn flow (Milestone 2b)
- **Turn start, production first.**
  - When a unit is completed at the start of your turn, the game says so and opens that city's production screen, so you can keep or change what it builds next.
  - It steps through each city that finished a unit.
- **Then units.** After production, the game steps through each unit that still has moves. The active unit **blinks** so it's obvious whose turn it is.
- **End of turn.**
  - When no units have moves left, the game says so and prompts you to end the turn.
  - The **End Turn** button blinks.

## Cargo auto-sentry (Milestone 2b, high priority)
- **Problem:** the unit cycle in 2a keeps offering armies riding a transport in open ocean, where they can't do anything.
- **Original rule:** armies on a transport may do nothing except unload.
- **Fix:**
  - A unit that boards a transport or carrier is automatically sentried.
  - It drops out of the unit cycle until woken.
  - It stays sentried, even while sailing along a coast, until the player clicks **Unload** on the transport.
  - The one automatic exception is the manual's rule: armies unload automatically when the transport enters a city that is the target of its move.
  - (Decided 2026-10-15. This replaces an earlier idea of waking cargo whenever the ship is next to land.)
- **Transports passing through cities:** on some maps a city is the gateway between two bodies of water.
  - If a transport on Go-to ends its turn inside a city that is not its destination, its armies stay aboard and asleep.
  - It carries on along its route next turn.
  - The armies unload only when:
    - the city is the transport's destination, or
    - the player moved the transport into the city by hand, with no further orders.
  - This replaces 2a's behavior of unloading at every friendly city.
- **Manual stop unloads next turn** (user refinement): when the player moves a transport into a city by hand, the armies unload at the **start of the next turn**, if the transport is still there with no orders. Until then they stay aboard, so a mid-route pause costs nothing.
- **Load/Unload buttons in cities:**
  - With a transport selected in a city, **Load** lets the player choose which armies in the city to put aboard. **Unload** releases all or chosen armies.
  - No need to walk units out and back in.
  - Today (2a), the only way to load in a city is the original's automatic rule: a transport leaving a city takes the armies there with it, up to capacity.
- **Keep the original auto-load by default.** It may also pick up armies meant as a garrison, so sentried/garrison armies may need to be excluded.
  - Check what the original did with sentried armies.
  - If our rule differs, it becomes a house-rule option.
- **Fighters on carriers:** under the original rules they may act normally. They're also auto-sentried on landing, because a landed fighter has ended its turn, and the player wakes them to launch.

## Standing orders (Milestone 2b, high priority)
- **No Sentry yet.** Units must be moved, skipped (S only lasts one turn) or disbanded every turn. That gets tedious fast.
- **Sentry** keeps a unit idle across turns until something happens nearby (an enemy is sighted) or the player wakes it.
- **Ship 2b's other orders with it:** Go-to, Patrol, Explore.
- **Superseded:** the earlier "Wait (Tab)" idea was merged into the original's **Skip (Space)**, which sends a unit to the back of this turn's queue. Tab became **Hold** ("done this turn"). See "Skip vs Wait" in the Orders menu section.
  - Tab must not move browser focus while the map is active.

## City screen on capture (Milestone 2b)
- **Problem:** 2a already selects a captured city in the side panel, but that's too subtle and the user didn't notice it.
- **Fix:** capturing a city immediately opens a prominent **city screen** (a dialog over the map) with:
  - name (editable)
  - production picker with build times
  - the city's units
  - a "Done" button
- **Reuse:** the same screen opens when you click your own city, and at turn start when the city finishes a unit.
- **Reference layout** (from an original-game screenshot shared 2026-10-16). Recreate the *function and simplicity* with our own modern styling, not the 1993 look.
  - **Header:** the city name, plus "Production".
  - **A "No Production" button** at the top.
  - **A list of unit types,** one row each, with:
    - the unit's icon and name (the current choice highlighted)
    - a **Turns to complete** column for this city, including the repeat-build bonus
    - an **In production** column: how many of your cities are currently building that type (empire-wide)
  - **Ships** appear only for coastal cities. (Hide them, or show them disabled with a tooltip.)
  - **Buttons:** "City" opens the city's details (name/rename, units inside, progress); "OK" closes.
  - **A thin leader line** from the dialog to the city on the map, so it's clear which city it is.
- **Modern touches:**
  - a progress bar for the current build
  - unit stats on hover (move, strength, range)
  - keyboard selection with arrow keys and Enter

## Unit action buttons (Milestone 2b)
- **Mouse-only play.** Every keyboard action needs a button.
- **Button bar:** when a unit is selected, show buttons for its actions in the unit panel or a toolbar:
  - Next, Skip, Sentry, Go-to, Center, Disband
  - Load/Unload when they apply
- **Behavior:**
  - Buttons that don't apply to the unit are hidden or disabled.
  - Each button shows its keyboard shortcut, e.g. "Next (N)".
- **Tablets:** the same buttons make the game playable on touch screens.

## Production report at turn start (Milestone 2b; reference screenshot 2026-10-16)
- **When:** at the start of your turn, a **Production Report** dialog pops up for each city that finished a unit. They come one after another, before you move any units.
- **Contents:**
  - "City <name> reports production of <a/an> <unit type>."
  - The new unit's generated name, e.g. "142nd Army", in an editable field with **Rename**.
  - **Prod.** opens that city's production screen, to change what it builds next.
  - **Help** explains the unit, with its stats.
  - **OK** moves on to the next city's report. After the last report, unit cycling begins.
- **Map:** the map centers on the city, with a leader line from the dialog to the city, as in the production screen.
- **This replaces the earlier idea** in "Turn flow" of jumping straight into the production screen. The report comes first, with Prod. one click away.
- **Style:** our own modern styling; only the function and flow come from the reference.

## Orders menu and original keyboard shortcuts (Milestone 2b; reference screenshot 2026-10-16)
- **Two places for orders:**
  - Keep the **buttons** for frequent actions (Next, Wait, Skip, Sentry, Move To).
  - Add an **Orders drop-down** (or right-click menu) for everything that applies to the selected unit. Orders that don't apply are greyed out, and each shows its shortcut.
- **The original's orders and keys.** Use the same keys, for familiarity:

  | Order | Key | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | Move To | T | Go-to with pathfinding |
  | Patrol | P | |
  | Go Home | H | Return to the nearest/home city |
  | Explore | X | |
  | Escort Ship | E | New order to add: follow and guard a ship |
  | Sentry | S | Was "skip" in 2a; moves to Space below |
  | Load Ship | L | |
  | Unload | U | |
  | Clear Orders | Ins | |
  | Skip/Continue | Space | Was "next unit" in 2a; Next becomes N only |
  | Disband Unit | Del | With a confirmation |

- **Our own additions:**
  - Next = **N**
  - Hold = **Tab** (see "Skip vs Wait" below)
  - Center = **F**
  - End Turn = **Enter**
- **Movement keys (decided 2026-10-16):**
  - Drop the letter movement keys (Q W E / A D / Z X C).
  - Move the selected unit with the **arrow keys**.
  - **Diagonals:** use the numpad (7 9 1 3), or the Home / PgUp / End / PgDn keys, the classic arrow-pad diagonals.
  - With no unit selected, the arrow keys pan the map. Mouse drag always pans.
- **Skip vs Wait (decided 2026-10-16):**
  - In the original, **Skip (Space)** sends the unit to the **back of this turn's queue**. Keep that, with the same key and meaning.
  - That makes the earlier "Wait" idea the same as Skip, so it merges into it.
  - Second option **Hold (Tab):** "done with this unit for this turn". It's 2a's old S behavior, which is no longer on S because S is now Sentry.
  - Final set:
    - **Space** = Skip (back of the queue)
    - **Tab** = Hold (done this turn)
    - **S** = Sentry (done until woken)
- **Other menus to check for later:** Reports, Commands, Display.

## Status Report (2b or later; reference screenshot 2026-10-16)
- **Where it opens:** from a **Reports** menu, as in the original's menu bar (File, Reports, Orders, Commands, Display). The manual's "Reports Menu" section (p.69) lists the original's reports. Check it for the others.
- **Contents, adapted to our rules:**
  - **Cities:** captured (owned), known (explored), and efficiency. Efficiency is an Advanced-rules value; show it once that exists.
  - **World explored:** % of the map explored.
  - **Combat efficiency:** a ratio of wins in our combats. Define the exact formula ourselves.
  - **Per unit type, one row each:**
    - under construction (cities building it)
    - soonest complete (turns)
    - in service (labelled "Are in Combat" in the original)
    - killed (enemy units of that type we destroyed)
    - lost (our units of that type destroyed)
- **Engine change:** add per-player **stats counters** to `GameState` (kills and losses by unit type, combats won and lost), updated by combat and destruction events. These also feed the Commander history and end-of-game screens later.
- **Privacy:** the report shows only the viewer's own numbers, plus counts of what they know (explored, known cities).

## Unit status line (2b; same screenshot)
- **Top status bar** for the selected unit, as in the original: "142nd Army, Rng: 1, Ord: None". That's name, range or moves, and current order.
- **Right side:** an **Orders** menu button and the turn number.
- Combine this with the unit panel / action-buttons design.

## Preferences screen (later polish; reference screenshot 2026-10-16)
- **What the original had:** a "Game Default Preferences" dialog with these groups:
  - **Production reports** on or off for Air, Land and Sea units. Lets the player silence the turn-start report for unit kinds they don't care about.
  - **Sound** toggles: player prompts, unit orders, battles/movement, warnings.
  - **Delays** (Slow/Medium/Fast): message display, battle animation, movement animation. These also cover our "watch AI turns" speed.
  - **Map scrolling** speed (Fast scroll on/off).
- **Our version:**
  - A small **Settings** screen, stored per player in browser storage. These are personal conveniences, not game rules, so they're separate from house rules.
  - Start with production-report filters, animation speed and sound volume/mute.
  - Add more only when a feature needs it.
- **Also visible in the screenshot:** the status line reads "Select starting location for Movement Path", with a **Paths** mode button. The original let you draw saved movement paths, probably reusable routes such as shipping lanes. Check the manual's Orders/Commands sections. It could fold into Go-to/Patrol in 2b.

## Movement (Milestone 2b, together with Go-to and pathfinding)
- **Path preview.** After selecting a unit, clicking or hovering a destination shows the planned path and how many turns it will take: this turn, or the turn number it arrives on.

## Message log (Milestone 2b)
- **Remove the list numbers** ("1.", "2.", "3."). They come from the log being an ordered list (`<ol>`), and they confuse people. Use a plain list.
- **Fewer turn lines.** Show the turn number only as a small header or divider, not a "Turn N: Player" line per turn. Showing every turn should be optional at most.

## Units inside cities (Milestone 2b, high priority)
- **New units are invisible on the map.** Units in a city aren't drawn; they're only listed in the city's side panel. Players don't notice new units.
  - Draw a unit badge on the city: the top unit's icon plus a count when several units are there.
  - A newly built unit should blink on top of its city, and be first in the turn's unit cycle.

## City names (Milestone 2b)
- **Real names, picked at random.** Each city gets a real-world city name at random, shown as a label on the map and in the city panel.
- **Editable.** A player can rename their own cities.
- **Implementation notes:**
  - Names are assigned by the map generator from the world seed, so a shared seed gives the same names.
  - They are stored as `City.name` in the game state; a captured city keeps its name.
  - Renaming is a new engine command, `renameCity`, allowed only for the current owner.
- **Name source:** city names are facts, so a list is free to use.
  - The simplest option is a curated list of about 500–1000 well-known names, kept in a data file in the repo.
  - If we pull from a dataset such as GeoNames (CC BY 4.0), credit it on an About/credits screen.
- **Region (decided):** the New Game screen gets an optional **name region** choice, e.g. World, Europe, North America, Asia. Leaving it unset picks names at random from the whole world list.
- **Also decide in 2b:**
  - Avoid duplicate names within one game.
  - Labels may need to hide at small zoom levels to avoid clutter.

## Cargo count on the map (Milestone 2b)
- **Show a number:** replace the small white "has cargo" square on transports and carriers with a **number badge** for the units aboard, e.g. "4".
- **Use the same badge style** for the unit count on cities (see "Units inside cities").
- **Readability:** the badge must stay legible at small zoom. At very small tile sizes, fall back to a dot.

## Save compatibility across builds (Milestone 2b)
- **2a saves will likely break.** 2b changes the game state (house rules, city names, unit names and orders) and some rules (cargo unloading).
  - A 2a save's replay check would fail under 2b, so the save would be rejected or would load incorrectly.
- **Starting with 2b:** add **save migrations**.
  - Each save records its format and rules version. Loading an older save upgrades it step by step and fills sensible defaults for new fields.
  - Old saves keep the rules they were started with. That fits the house-rules design.
  - The replay check uses the save's own rules version. Saves that can't be fully verified are loaded from their snapshot, with a "loaded from older version" note.
- **Whether to migrate 2a saves too:** only if cheap. Otherwise tell players that 2a saves are not supported.

## Map view (Milestone 2b)
- **Dynamic zoom.** Early on only a few squares are explored, so the screen is mostly black.
  - Auto-zoom to fit the explored area (plus a margin) so the playable part fills the view.
  - Zoom out step by step as more of the map is explored.
  - If the player zooms manually, keep their zoom until they reset it (e.g. a "fit" button or key).
  - This likely needs a larger maximum tile size than today's 48 px for the opening turns.

## Unit info panel (Milestone 2b)
- **Prominent active-unit panel.** The right-hand panel prominently shows the active (blinking) unit and whose turn it is.
- **Fields to show:**
  - unit type with a picture
  - a name
  - health
  - range/fuel
  - moves
  - cargo
- **Names.** Each unit gets a generated military-style name, such as "151st Infantry" instead of "Army #1". Optionally, players can give a unit their own name.
  - Generated names can be derived from the unit id and type, so they stay deterministic.
  - Custom names would need a new `renameUnit` command and a `name` field in state.
- **Clearer health.** "Hits 1/1" is confusing. Show damage in a clearer way, e.g. "Damage 0/1", or a health bar labelled with the unit's strength.

## Title screen layout (quick cosmetic win; do early in 2b)
- **Problem:** the key art sits in a 760 px column, leaving wide black margins on desktop.
- **Fix:**
  - Make the art a **full-screen background**: `background-size: cover`, anchored so the baked-in title stays visible.
  - Float the menus (Continue, New game, Saved games) in a translucent panel over the art, e.g. on the left over the consoles area or as a side column, so they don't cover the logo.
- **Tall or narrow screens and phones:** fall back to the art on top with the menus stacked below.
- **Blank edges:** if `cover` crops too much on ultra-wide screens, fill the edges with a blurred, darkened copy of the art.

## Effects and sound (later: polish/art pass)
- **Battle animations:** at minimum, units slide between squares, there's a muzzle flash or explosion on combat, and a sinking or explosion effect when a unit is destroyed.
  - The main spec already calls for a brief combat animation.
  - 2a shows combat only in the message log.
- **Reference from the original** (screenshot 2026-10-16):
  - During an attack, an **explosion graphic flashes on the target square** (the city), next to the attacker.
  - The **top status line narrates the action**, e.g. "142nd Army is attacking Neutral City Yalu River.", then shows the result.
- **Cheap first version for 2b:**
  - status-line narration using unit names and city names
  - a short explosion flash on the defender's square, lasting about as long as the "Battle delay" preference
  - then the result in the status line and the log
  - The full animation and sound come in the polish pass.
- **Sound:** combat, unit built, city captured, turn start; plus a mute/volume control. Sound is on the main spec's "later" list.
- **Per-unit movement sounds:** each unit type has its own sound when it moves, as in the original game. Examples: marching boots, a tank engine, a jet whoosh, a ship's engine or horn.
  - Sounds must be our own: newly made, generated, or openly licensed. None may be taken from the original game.

## Art (a later art pass)
- **City icon** that looks like a city (buildings or skyline in the owner's color), not a plain colored square.
- **Unit icons** that look like the unit: an infantry figure for Army, plane silhouettes, ship silhouettes, and so on, instead of letters.
