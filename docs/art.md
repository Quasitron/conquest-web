# Artwork

## Unit cards

`public/art/<unit>.webp` are the artwork cards shown on the production screen (beside the list of units) and on the production report. They are 560 px versions of the originals, which are kept outside the project in `Documents\Claude\art-originals` (1024 px, one per unit, including Armor, Bomber and Spy for the Advanced rules).

- **Made with:** Gemini (AI image generation), by the game's owner, in the style of the intro page.
- **The made-up lettering:** every card has nonsense text baked into its top-left corner (a usual look of AI-generated lettering). The game covers it with a dark panel (`.unit-card-hud` in `src/ui/styles.css`) that lists the unit's real numbers (`cardLines` in `src/ui/labels.ts`), so the pictures themselves are unchanged.
- **Adding a unit:** make `public/art/<type>.webp` (the same recipe: scale to 560 px, WebP quality about 82) and the card appears; the test `tests/ui/unitCards.test.ts` checks that every unit type has one.
- **Map icons** are separate: the cards are too busy for a map square, so a later art pass would add small simplified icons.
## Map icons

`public/art/units/<unit>.webp` are the pictures drawn on the map when the squares are big enough (28 px and up, `ICON_MIN_TILE` in `src/ui/unitIcons.ts`); zoomed out, a unit is still its letter badge. They are made from the pictures in `Documents\Claude\art-originals\units` by `tools/make-unit-icons.ps1` (run it from the project folder): the plain background is removed by flood-filling inward from the picture's edges (so light patches inside the unit stay), the unit is trimmed and scaled to at most 320 px.

- **Adding a unit's picture:** drop it in `art-originals\units`, add it to the `$units` list in the script (and to `UNIT_ICONS` in `src/ui/unitIcons.ts`, with the way it faces), run the script. A picture that needs a stray line cleared at an edge can be listed in `$ignoreRight`.
- **Facing:** each picture records which way it faces (`faces`). A unit is drawn mirrored when its last move was the other way (`unitsHeadingLeft`, from the move events in the log, so after a reload everyone faces right until it moves; enemy units are not mirrored). In a fight the two units face each other. A picture with `faces: null` (the Army) never flips.
- **Drawing:** a thick border in the owner's colour with a dark outline, and a light dark underlay behind the picture (no colour wash inside: red over grass looked brown) (`unitMark` in `src/ui/render.ts`).
- **Photo-real pictures (current):** the map pictures are now photo-real, replacing the first cartoon set (2026-10-24). Rules for a new picture: one unit on a plain white background, no water, wake or reflection (the script keys out the background, not water), the bow or nose pointing right (every picture faces right now), and a view from slightly above rather than a flat side profile, so ships do not shrink to slivers at map size. Ask the AI tool for a distinctive colour for ships that must be told apart (all warships are grey).
- **Source file names:** save the picture in `art-originals\units` as `<Name>-v2`, `-v3`, `-v4` or `-v5` (the script uses the highest version it finds, up to `-v5`; add `'v6'` to the list in the script for more). **The script cannot read `.webp`**: convert a `.webp` to PNG first (`ffmpeg -i X-v3.webp X-v3.png`, keeping the original as `X-v3-orig.webp`; an unconverted file gives an empty or missing picture). A picture with water left in it needs the water cropped off before converting.
- **Airbase:** `public/art/units/airbase.webp` is a plain crop of `art-originals\units\Airfield.webp` (the airfield inside its fence, 300 px wide; ffmpeg `crop=666:714:291:57,scale=300:-2`), not keyed, so it is drawn as an opaque tile (`faces: null`). The script does not make it. The Bomber's card (`public/art/bomber.webp`) and map icon are in the game.
- **Ready but not in the game (Spy; Armor and Bomber are now in):** `bomber.webp`, `armor.webp` and `spy.webp` (for the Advanced rules; all face right, the Spy is seen from behind looking right). Every unit in the game has a picture. Each picture has a `size` in `UNIT_ICONS` (how much of the square it may fill) so the bigger ships are drawn bigger. The script also drops stray specks left in a picture's background, and has a `$rotate` table (empty now) for a picture drawn on a slant.
- **Victory and Defeated cards:** `public/art/victory.webp` and `defeat.webp`, shown full screen when a human (Victory) or the computer (Defeat) wins. The garbled corner text of the originals was painted out with ffmpeg (`delogo` plus a blurred patch over the region x 132..272, y 120..216 of the 1024 px image); redo that for any new card.- **The city:** `city.webp` (made from `City.jpg` by the same script) is drawn for every city when squares are 28 px or bigger: the skyline picture standing on a band in the owner's colour, and a flag at the tip of its aerial; there is no colour wash behind it, as red over grass looked brown (`drawCity` in `src/ui/render.ts`). Until the picture has loaded, and on smaller squares, a code-drawn skyline is used; a city nobody owns has a grey band and no flag.

## Explosions

`public/art/fx/explosion-1..3.webp` (fire) and `smoke-1..2.webp` are drawn over the loser of a fight that destroys a unit or takes a city (`src/ui/explosions.ts`, called from `drawMap` in `src/ui/render.ts`). The code burst is used for a plain hit, and until the pictures have loaded.

- **Fire** is drawn as light (`globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`), so the black round it disappears and its edges stay soft. Made from `art-originals\Explosion-1..3.webp`: cropped square round the burst, near-black haze cut off with a levels curve (ffmpeg `lutrgb`), 512 px.
- **Smoke** is a normal alpha picture behind the fire. Made from `Smoke-1..2.webp` (dark cloud on a pale backdrop with a shadow): darkness becomes opacity (`lutyuv` into `alphamerge`), so the backdrop and shadow go transparent.
- **Timing:** the fire flashes in, swells and settles at half strength; it starts dimming halfway through the burst (explosionFade) and is at half strength when the fight ends, then lingers 0.35 s (EXPLOSION_TAIL_MS) fading out. Which picture a fight uses is fixed by its square and attacker, so the same fight always looks the same.
- **Not used:** `Explosion-seq.webp` (four side-on panels ending as mushroom clouds, which is more like a bomb than a sunk ship).

## Terrain pictures

The ground is drawn from five photo-real pictures, `public/art/terrain/water|clear|rough|forest|mountain.webp` (936 x 624 px, about 650 KB in all), made by `tools/make-terrain-tiles.ps1` from the pictures the owner chose in `Documents\Claude\art-originals\terrain`: Water-A, Clear-C, Rough-C, Forest-C and Mountain-C (the spec that went to the artist is `docs/terrain-art-spec.md`; the other options are kept there too). The script levels the lighting (a heavy blur is subtracted), makes each picture seamless (blended with itself shifted by half a picture, feathered at the edges), moves its colour to a target average, and shrinks it. Rerun it if a picture is replaced.

- **How it is drawn** (`src/ui/terrainTiles.ts`): each picture is one continuous patch; a map square takes the 78 px cell at its own position (x mod 12, y mod 8), so neighbouring squares of the same ground join up and the picture repeats every 12 x 8 squares. Smaller than 12 px a square is just the picture's flat colour (`FLAT`). The pictures also live as 1/2, 1/3 and 1/6 size copies, drawn from the nearest one, which keeps panning fast.
- **Blends:** from 24 px, squares of different ground fade into each other over their shared edges and corners. From 30 px each fade is cut from the neighbour's picture at the square's own place, so the join is seamless; below 30 px a ready-made sprite per ground, side and size is used. The sea meets the land through the shore picture instead of a fade (next item).
- **Rivers** are curves through the middle of each river square, bent a little at the edges (the same for both neighbours), stroked in the sea picture with a darker bank.
- **Games without terrain** draw every land square as Clear, so the pictures are used in all games, not only the Advanced rules.
- **Colours:** the pictures were darker than the old flat colours, so the script lifts them: water 12,72,112 (the red channel is kept low, a bigger lift tinted the sparkles pink), clear 95,143,74, rough 140,118,80, forest 38,78,46, mountain 122,124,130.
- **The shore** (`public/art/terrain/shore.webp`, 624 x 170 px with transparency, 14 KB): a strip of beach cut from `Shore-D` (rows 300..640 of the 1248 x 832 original) by the same script: made seamless left to right, its colour calmed (saturation x0.75), and its top and bottom edges faded to transparent so it lays over the sea and the grass. Along a coast it repeats every 4 squares. The foam line falls on the edge between a sea square and a land square; the sea square shows the shallows (0.58 of a square deep), the land square the sand (0.5 deep). `terrainTiles.ts` makes four copies (flipped or turned for the sea lying north, east, south or west) and cuts each square's piece by its map position along the coast, so neighbours join. Below 36 px it is drawn at 70% so coasts are not outlined too boldly. Chosen over Shore-A/B/C (A and B too bright a turquoise against the sea, C's wavy foam repeats visibly).
